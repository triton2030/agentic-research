# Designs of pages and app screens

Read before a brief for a landing page, an app screen or one block of it.
The lessons come from one series of about 120 images of a dark dashboard at
`high` (2026-11), one run each unless counted; when a new series confirms or
contradicts a line, update that line.

## When you choose what to give the model

| Input | Use it for | What it gave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text only | a screen that does not exist yet | A plausible dashboard from about 100 words; unnamed slots filled with invented blocks, slogans, numbers and claims; a content list's order became the page's order. |
| The original screenshot | restyling or rearranging a live screen | A brief of 40–100 words about what changes; without style words the look stays. |
| The original and one reference | a live screen in a new look | The reference's color, light and material on the screenshot's layout (6 of 6). |
| The original and references, each with its role | one thing from each: one's gradient, another's material | Look roles held: `only how its gradient is made, for <the main card>` put that gradient on that card alone; `only its materials and light` brought glass and glowing edges (2 of 2); `never its colors` kept the palette out (3 of 3). `only its structure, how its blocks are arranged` carried no layout (0 of 3): name the layout in a `Composition:` sentence. |
| References only | a new screen in a borrowed look | The reference's look on the model's own layout, nothing of the reference's content copied (2 of 2); labels invented from the brief. |

A reference brings its palette: a hot one repainted the page despite
`Everything is dark and cold` (2 of 2); cold and neutral ones kept it (4 of
4). With a main reference and five "mood only" extras, `tiny 3D icons of
clear cut ice` grew into big ice cubes in 4 of 10 despite `No pictures or
scenery`.

## When you write the brief

Roles first, then one sentence per change, then the one invariant and the
frame:

```text
Image 1 is the original screenshot of <the screen> of our <product> web app: keep its content. Image 2 is the main reference: redesign Image 1 in its style and spirit, never copying its content or pictures.

Composition: <one idea of layout>.

Everything is dark and cold; only <the main action> is hot, with an orange-to-red gradient.

No pictures or scenery. Flat screenshot of the app window only.
```

- A `Composition:` sentence moved the layout as named in 16 of 18; without
  one the screenshot's layout stayed in 7 of 8, and `rearrange and regroup
  its parts freely` moved it in 2 of 8. Worked: a large card beside slim
  stacked rows; the main card in the centre, raised; numbered steps joined by
  a thin line; a row of tall tiles; a bento grid; full-width list rows; one
  panel split by thin dividers; a panel with a switcher; two halves, what you
  can do and what you can check. Flat color blocks and a narrow phone-like
  column did not.
- A design idea takes one sentence too: `Design: instead of icons, abstract
  gradient illustrations` gave each tile its own artwork.
- Empty space beside a headline draws a big picture (4 of 5, twice), and so
  do scene words: `fog`, `atmospheric`, `softly lit`, `3D render`,
  `translucent ice`. `It is a dashboard, not a landing page.` left at most a
  faint silhouette; `No pictures or scenery anywhere.` removed it (2 of 2).
- A childish look left with words about surfaces and type: `matte, tonal …
  low contrast and no glow`, `hairline borders`, `light and regular weights`,
  `like a private bank's own trading tool`, `Nothing playful`, heat as a
  `filling` rather than a glow (3 of 3). "Trendy" came from named techniques,
  a mesh gradient with fine grain or smoked glass, not from the word.
- Words about the users set the hierarchy and become page text: naming who
  it is for and which action comes first put that action first and hottest
  (3 of 3); `how much they hold` relabeled network numbers as the user's
  balance (3 of 3); a thing to verify, `a public reserve they can check`, grew
  its own panel (3 of 4). To keep labels: `Keep every label and number exactly
  as in Image 1 and add none: no slogans, taglines or badges.` (3 of 3);
  without `add none`, invented slogans came back (4 of 4).

## When you set the frame and size

- Type size follows the input's scale, not words: a 1118-px screenshot on a
  2352-px canvas kept its large type under `smaller type` (3 of 3), so give a
  wider screenshot. From text, `the whole page at 100% scale on a wide
  1920-pixel desktop screen` with `modest, refined type sizes` made type
  smaller and margins wider (4 of 4).
- For one block, screenshot only that block at double pixel density and end
  with `Flat screenshot of just this block of the app.`: 12 of 12 showed the
  block alone, at $0.05–0.08 for 2048×1152 against $0.12–0.14 for a page at
  2352×3520.

## When the image arrives

The model fills what the layout lacks: a switcher panel got an invented
exchange form, a list got arrows instead of buttons. Long rows of chart
numbers got wrong digits, $321,000.00 as $521,000.00 twice: check the
numbers that matter.
