# Red Flags

- Do not launch first and explain later.
- Do not let two workers share ownership of the same files.
- Do not delegate the main agent's immediate blocking next step.
- Do not hand already-dirty hotspot files to workers unless the brief names the dirty state and isolates a narrow delta.
- Do not trust a worker status message before checking the on-disk diff.
- Do not ask clarifying questions that would not change the split or the brief.
- Do not write broad briefs that invite drift.
- Do not turn a subagent brief into a rigid algorithm when role, task, and quality criteria would preserve more useful judgment.
- Do not omit explicit allowed and forbidden boundaries when the worker has freedom inside the task.
- Do not replace a clear role card and success criteria with a long procedural checklist unless the sequence is truly load-bearing.
- Do not let a worker claim verification outside the worker's scoped checks.
- Do not let a worker report beyond owned scope or summarize adjacent workers.
- Do not let a worker turn into a mini-orchestrator by assigning, narrating, or speaking for other workers.
- Do not confuse scope discipline with forced brevity when the owned deliverable is analysis, critique, or strategy prose.
- Do not let launcher or tool availability commentary crowd out the owned task unless it is a real blocker.
- Do not force a "launch or not?" confirmation pause unless the user explicitly asked for plan-first review.
- Do not turn an auditor into a shadow owner that re-decides strategy, architecture, and task criteria.
- Do not ask an auditor for final sign-off when there is no artifact-level evidence to inspect.
- Do not paste every launch brief into chat by default; keep them internal unless the user asked to inspect them.
- Do not quietly switch to some other execution flow while framing this as native subagent preparation.
- Do not treat a current-reality snapshot as proof that the work is already done.
- Do not ignore a real current-reality signal when it names the remaining gap that should drive the split.
- Do not collapse preexisting unrelated failures into the verdict on the delegated scope.
